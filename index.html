Gradient descent, visually

To find the bottom of a valley in the fog, feel which way is uphill and step the other way.

Fitting a line by walking downhill

Goal: choose the intercept c0 and slope c1 of the line ŷ = c0 + c1x with the smallest mean squared error (MSE).

Approach: start anywhere. The gradient ∇MSE points the way MSE rises fastest, so take a small step against it, and repeat.

MSE(c) = 1n ∑i (yi − c0 − c1xi)²
∂MSE∂c0 = −2n ∑i ri ∂MSE∂c1 = −2n ∑i rixi c ← c − η ∇MSE(c)

The error ri = yi − ŷi is the red segment from sample i to the line. Every c = (c0, c1) is one line (right) and one point on the error surface (left); η is the step size.

How big a step?

Goal: find the c that minimizes f(c).

Approach: the gradient points uphill, so a step against it goes down. The step size η sets how far: too small and it crawls, too large and it overshoots the bottom, or even climbs out.

c ← c − η ∇f(c) parabola: f(c) = a(c − c*)²

The 2D bowl is steepest along one axis (curvature 1) and shallowest along the other (curvature 1/κ). The condition number κ says how stretched the bowl is: at κ = 1 it is round.

Try this

  • Click far from the bottom and press Gradient step a few times. Which way does the orange step arrow point, compared with the contour lines?
    AnswerPerpendicular to the contour line through c: straight downhill from where you stand. That is usually not straight at the minimum.
  • Drag the c tip along the bold contour line. MSE stays the same, but the line on the right changes. Find two very different lines with the same MSE.
  • Drag c in a small circle around the bottom. Which way does MSE rise fastest? Turn on Show gradient to check.
    AnswerAlong the gradient (dashed arrow), at right angles to the contour line.
  • Raise η until the walk diverges. Then turn on Show step-size zones: where did it break?
  • On Uncentered, press Run. Why is the valley so long and thin?
    AnswerThe data sit far from x = 0. Raising the slope while lowering the intercept pivots the line about the middle of the data, which barely changes MSE: that pair of moves is the valley floor. Switch x to Centered and run again.
  • On Uneven scales, find the largest η that converges. How many steps does it take? Switch x to Standardized and try again.
  • On Outlier, turn on Show min and drag the outlier. Why does one point move the best line so much?
    AnswerErrors are squared: a sample twice as far off costs four times as much.
  • Drag the line's square handles until it matches the dashed best line. Watch your c walk to the ★ on the surface.
  • On the parabola, is f′(c) positive or negative to the right of the bottom? Which way does the step go?
  • Find the η that lands at the bottom in one step. What is it, in terms of a?
    Answerη = 1/(2a): the step −η · 2a(c − c*) is then exactly −(c − c*).
  • Pick an η that zig-zags, then one that blows up. Now raise the curvature a: the same η is no longer safe.
  • On Two valleys, start on the right. Find one η that ends in the right valley and another that ends in the left.
  • On the 2D Stretched bowl, find the largest η that converges and count the steps. Why does it zig-zag across the valley but crawl along it?
    AnswerOne η serves both directions. It must be small enough for the steep direction (or it diverges), which makes it tiny for the shallow one.
  • Rotate the bowl with θ. Does the number of steps change?
    AnswerNo. Gradient descent depends only on the bowl's shape, not which way it faces: the path just turns with it.
